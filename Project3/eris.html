Eris

Eris is the most massive known dwarf planet, and its discovery in 2005 pushed astronomers to define what a planet is.

Eris is about 27% more massive than Pluto, though Pluto is slightly wider. It orbits far beyond Neptune on a long, stretched path that takes about 560 Earth years to complete.

Surface and Moon

Eris is a mix of rock and ice. Its surface is thought to be covered in frozen methane that reflects most of the sunlight reaching it, and temperatures stay near −240 °C.

Its only known moon, Dysnomia, is about 150 kilometers wide and circles Eris every 16 days. Eris is named for the Greek goddess of discord, and Dysnomia for her daughter.

Orbit and Rotation

Eris's orbit carries it from about 38 times Earth's distance from the Sun to nearly 100 times. It is currently near the far end of that path.

Its orbit is also tilted about 44 degrees from the flat plane in which the planets orbit.

Discovery and Observation

Mike Brown and his team found Eris in images taken at the Palomar Observatory in California. They nicknamed it Xena before it received its official name in 2006.

No spacecraft has visited Eris. It is about three times farther from the Sun than Pluto, so a mission would take a very long time.
