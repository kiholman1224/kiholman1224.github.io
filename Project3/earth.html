Earth

Earth is the third planet from the Sun, the largest of the rocky planets, and the only world known to support life.

About 71% of Earth's surface is covered by liquid water, which gives the planet its blue color when seen from space. Its atmosphere, a thin layer of mostly nitrogen and oxygen, shields life from harmful radiation.

Surface and Atmosphere

Earth's atmosphere is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen. Its magnetic field and ozone layer protect the surface from the Sun's charged particles and ultraviolet light.

The surface is constantly reshaped by moving tectonic plates, volcanoes, weather, and water. These forces keep the planet's crust far younger than the surfaces of Mercury or the Moon.

Orbit and Rotation

Earth circles the Sun in about 365.25 days, which is why we add a leap day to the calendar every four years.

The planet's axis is tilted by about 23.4 degrees. That tilt, not the distance from the Sun, causes the changing seasons.

Observing Earth

In December 1972, the Apollo 17 crew photographed the whole Earth in the image known as the Blue Marble. NASA's Landsat satellites have monitored the planet's land since 1972.

Astronauts have continuously lived aboard the International Space Station since November 2000, studying Earth from about 400 kilometers above the surface.
