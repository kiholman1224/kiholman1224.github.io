Earth's Moon

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and the fifth-largest moon in the solar system. It is the only world beyond Earth that humans have visited.

Most scientists think the Moon formed about 4.5 billion years ago, when a Mars-sized object struck the young Earth and the debris joined together to form the Moon.

Surface

The Moon's surface is covered in craters from billions of years of impacts. The dark patches, called maria, are plains of ancient lava, and the brighter regions are older highlands.

The Moon has no true atmosphere. Spacecraft have found water ice inside permanently shadowed craters near its poles.

Orbit and Rotation

The Moon orbits Earth every 27.3 days and rotates at the same rate, so the same side always faces us. Its cycle of phases repeats every 29.5 days.

Its gravity pulls on Earth's oceans and causes the tides. The Moon is also drifting away from Earth by about 3.8 centimeters each year.

Exploration

The Soviet Luna 2 reached the Moon in 1959, and Luna 3 photographed its far side the same year. Apollo 11 landed the first humans on July 20, 1969, and twelve astronauts walked on the Moon before Apollo 17 left in 1972.

In April 2026, the four Artemis II astronauts flew around the Moon, the first crew to do so since Apollo 17. NASA currently plans a crewed landing on Artemis IV in 2028.
