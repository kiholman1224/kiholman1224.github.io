Europa

Europa is an icy moon of Jupiter that hides a global ocean of salty water beneath its frozen surface. Scientists consider it one of the most promising places to look for life beyond Earth.

It is slightly smaller than Earth's Moon, with a bright, smooth surface crossed by long cracks and ridges.

Ice and Ocean

Europa's ice shell is thought to be about 15 to 25 kilometers thick, with an ocean underneath that may hold more than twice as much water as all of Earth's oceans combined. That ocean probably touches a rocky seafloor.

Europa has one of the smoothest surfaces in the solar system, with few craters, which suggests it is young and constantly renewed. Hubble Space Telescope observations have hinted at plumes of water vapor erupting from the surface.

Orbit and Heating

Europa orbits Jupiter every 3.55 Earth days and keeps one face toward the planet. Its orbit is slightly stretched, so Jupiter's gravity flexes the moon and warms its interior.

The surface stays far below freezing, around −160 °C, so any liquid water lies deep beneath the ice.

Exploration

NASA's Galileo spacecraft, which orbited Jupiter from 1995 to 2003, found magnetic signals that point to a salty ocean under the ice.

NASA's Europa Clipper launched in October 2024 and is expected to reach Jupiter in 2030. It will study Europa during dozens of close flybys.
