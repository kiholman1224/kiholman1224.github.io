Io

Io is the most volcanically active world in the solar system, with more than 400 active volcanoes erupting across its surface.

It is the innermost of Jupiter's four large moons and is slightly larger than Earth's Moon. Sulfur paints its surface in shades of yellow, orange, and red.

Volcanoes and Surface

Io's volcanoes blast plumes of sulfur dioxide as high as 500 kilometers above the surface, and lava lakes such as Loki Patera spread across the ground. The constant eruptions bury craters, so Io has almost none.

The moon has only a very thin atmosphere, made mostly of sulfur dioxide from its volcanoes.

Orbit and Heating

Io circles Jupiter every 1.77 Earth days and always keeps the same face toward the planet.

Jupiter's powerful gravity, along with the pull of its neighbors Europa and Ganymede, squeezes and stretches Io's interior. That tidal heating melts rock and fuels the volcanoes.

Exploration

Voyager 1 discovered Io's volcanoes in 1979, the first active volcanoes ever seen beyond Earth. NASA's Galileo spacecraft flew past Io several times between 1995 and 2003.

NASA's Juno made two very close flybys in December 2023 and February 2024, passing within about 1,500 kilometers of the surface.
