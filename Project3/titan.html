Titan

Titan is Saturn's largest moon and the second-largest moon in the solar system, bigger than the planet Mercury. It is the only moon with a thick atmosphere.

Its orange haze hides a surface with rivers, lakes, and seas, but they are filled with liquid methane and ethane instead of water.

Atmosphere and Surface

Titan's atmosphere is mostly nitrogen with a little methane, and the air pressure at the surface is about 1.5 times Earth's. Methane rain fills lakes and seas near the poles, including a large one named Kraken Mare.

Dunes of dark, sand-like grains stretch across the equator. Scientists think an ocean of liquid water lies beneath the icy crust.

Orbit and Rotation

Titan orbits Saturn every 15.9 Earth days and always shows the same face to the planet.

At about −179 °C, Titan is cold enough that methane behaves the way water does on Earth, forming clouds, rain, and lakes.

Exploration

Cassini flew past Titan more than 100 times between 2004 and 2017. Its Huygens probe landed on January 14, 2005, the first landing in the outer solar system.

NASA's Dragonfly, a drone-like rotorcraft, is currently planned to launch in 2028 and reach Titan in 2034.
