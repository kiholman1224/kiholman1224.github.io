Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system, a gas giant more than twice as massive as all the other planets combined.

It has no solid surface. Bands of hydrogen and helium clouds swirl around the planet, and a storm called the Great Red Spot is larger than Earth.

Atmosphere and Structure

Jupiter is made mostly of hydrogen and helium, like the Sun. Deeper in, the pressure is so high that the gas behaves like a liquid, and scientists think a dense core may sit at the center.

Fast winds stretch the clouds into colorful bands. The Great Red Spot is a storm that astronomers have observed for at least 150 years, and it has been slowly shrinking.

Orbit and Rotation

Jupiter takes almost 12 Earth years to orbit the Sun, yet it spins faster than any other planet, completing a rotation in under 10 hours.

That fast spin flattens the planet slightly and helps drive the strongest magnetic field of any planet.

Exploration

Pioneer 10 made the first flyby in 1973, and Voyagers 1 and 2 followed in 1979. NASA's Galileo orbited the planet from 1995 to 2003 and dropped a probe into its atmosphere.

NASA's Juno arrived in 2016 to study Jupiter's interior and magnetic field. Europa Clipper and ESA's JUICE are on their way and are expected to arrive in the early 2030s.
