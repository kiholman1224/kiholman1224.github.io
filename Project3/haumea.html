Haumea

Haumea is an oval-shaped dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt, stretched into the shape of a football by its extremely fast spin.

It rotates about once every four hours, one of the fastest spins of any large object in the solar system. Its two moons, Hi'iaka and Namaka, are named for daughters of the Hawaiian goddess Haumea.

Shape and Surface

Haumea is a rocky world covered by a bright coating of water ice, which makes it very reflective. Its fast rotation stretches it into a long, flattened oval.

In 2017, astronomers watching Haumea pass in front of a distant star discovered that it has a ring, the first ring system found around a Kuiper Belt object.

Orbit and Moons

Haumea takes about 285 Earth years to orbit the Sun. Its day is only about four hours long.

Scientists think a giant collision billions of years ago may have set Haumea spinning and created its two moons, Hi'iaka and Namaka.

Observing Haumea

No spacecraft has visited Haumea, so everything we know comes from telescope observations. The ring was found by timing the starlight Haumea blocked as it passed in front of the star.

The International Astronomical Union officially recognized Haumea as a dwarf planet in 2008.
