Neptune

Neptune is the most distant planet from the Sun, a deep blue ice giant about 30 times farther from the Sun than Earth.

It was the first planet found by mathematical prediction. Astronomers in Berlin spotted it in 1846 after calculations showed where it should be.

Atmosphere and Structure

Like Uranus, Neptune is an ice giant made mostly of water, ammonia, and methane around a rocky core. Methane gives it a rich blue color.

Its winds are the fastest in the solar system, reaching more than 2,000 kilometers per hour. Voyager 2 saw a huge dark storm there in 1989 that had vanished by the time the Hubble Space Telescope looked in 1994.

Orbit and Rotation

Neptune takes about 165 Earth years to circle the Sun. It completed its first full orbit since its discovery in 2011.

A day on Neptune lasts about 16 hours.

Exploration

Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to visit Neptune, flying past in August 1989. It revealed rings, new moons, and geysers on Neptune's largest moon, Triton.

Triton orbits backward and is probably a captured object from the Kuiper Belt, the icy region beyond Neptune.
