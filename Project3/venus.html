Venus

Venus is the hottest planet in the solar system and Earth's closest neighbor in size. It is nearly as wide as Earth, but the two worlds could hardly be more different.

A thick blanket of carbon dioxide traps heat so effectively that the surface stays around 465 °C, hot enough to melt lead. Clouds of sulfuric acid hide the ground from view.

Surface and Atmosphere

Venus's atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide, and the air pressure at the surface is about 90 times greater than on Earth. Standing there would feel like being deep under the ocean.

Beneath the clouds lie vast volcanic plains, thousands of volcanoes, and mountain ranges. Maxwell Montes, the tallest mountain, rises about 11 kilometers above the surrounding terrain.

Orbit and Rotation

Venus rotates in the opposite direction from most planets, so on Venus the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east. It also spins so slowly that one rotation takes longer than its 225-day year.

A solar day, from one sunrise to the next, lasts about 117 Earth days.

Exploration

The Soviet Venera 7 probe landed on Venus in 1970 and became the first spacecraft to transmit data from the surface of another planet. The harsh heat and pressure destroyed Venus landers within about two hours.

NASA's Magellan spacecraft used radar to map nearly the entire surface between 1990 and 1994, and new missions are being planned for the 2030s.
