Mars

Mars is a cold, dry desert world about half the size of Earth. Rust-colored iron dust gives it its red appearance.

Mars has the tallest volcano and one of the longest canyons in the solar system. Its landscape also shows signs that rivers and lakes once flowed there billions of years ago.

Surface and Atmosphere

Olympus Mons, an ancient volcano roughly 22 kilometers tall, stands about two and a half times higher than Mount Everest. Valles Marineris, a canyon system, stretches more than 4,000 kilometers across the planet.

The atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide and is less than 1% as dense as Earth's. Ice caps cover both poles, and water ice also lies beneath the surface.

Orbit and Rotation

A Mars day is only about 40 minutes longer than an Earth day, but its year lasts 687 Earth days.

Mars is tilted about 25 degrees, close to Earth's tilt, so it has seasons too. Each season lasts roughly twice as long as one on Earth.

Exploration

Mars is the most explored planet beyond Earth. NASA's Sojourner rover landed in 1997, followed by Spirit and Opportunity in 2004, Curiosity in 2012, and Perseverance in 2021.

Perseverance has been exploring Jezero Crater, a dry lake bed, and collecting rock samples that scientists hope to study on Earth someday. Its small helicopter companion, Ingenuity, made the first powered flights on another planet.
